How it works

Hot Seat Poker is a live table for teaching. The teacher controls the cards and the game; students sit in seats and make their own decisions. This guide walks through a study session from start to finish.

Want to look around first? On the table page, choose Open practice table. It runs on your device only, with every seat yours to play.

Start a lobby

A lobby is one table that stays open between sessions. Its cards, stacks and settings are saved, so you can pick up next week where you stopped.

  1. Open the table and type your name under You.
  2. Under Start a lobby, give it a name your students will recognize, like “Tuesday study group”.
  3. Choose a teacher code. Anyone with this code can see every card and run the table, so share it only with co-teachers. Write it down: there is no way to recover it.
  4. Press Create lobby. You're now at the table as a teacher.

Next time, this browser remembers your code: press Rejoin as teacher next to the lobby. On another device, press Teach and enter the code.

Bring in students

Students need only the lobby's name. They open hotseatpoker.com/play, type their name, press Join as student on your lobby, and tap an open seat. The People tab on your side panel also has a direct link to paste into a group chat.

Co-teachers press Teach on the lobby and enter the teacher code. You'll see each other's pointers, and a colored outline shows what the other teacher is editing.

Set up the table

Everything about the game lives in the Table setup tab:

  • Seats, blinds and stacks. Pick 2 to 9 seats, set the small and big blind, and use Set every stack to to reset everyone at once. Individual stacks and names are on the Seats tab.
  • Antes. Everyone antes, or a big blind ante.
  • Straddle and bomb pots. A straddle every hand, or just the next hand from the action bar. A bomb pot sends everyone straight to the flop for a set amount.
  • Rake. A percentage with an optional cap, and “no flop, no drop” if your room plays it.
  • All-ins and timing. Run it twice when players are all in, and an optional shot clock. When time runs out the player checks if they can, otherwise folds.

Use Sit out on the Seats tab for an empty chair, and Button here to move the button between hands.

Choose the cards

This is the heart of teaching with Hot Seat Poker: set up exactly the hand you want to discuss.

  1. Between hands, click any card slot, on the table or in the Seats tab. A card picker opens.
  2. Pick cards for that player, then move to the next slot. Random card fills a slot from the deck; Clear slot leaves it for the deal.
  3. Board cards work the same way. Turn and river cards you set show faded until that street is dealt.
  4. Any slot you leave empty is dealt at random when you deal.

During a hand you can still change a card: the picker swaps it in place. To replay the same hole cards, turn on Keep my card setup for the next hand in Table setup.

Play a hand

  1. Press Deal hand.
  2. When it's a student's turn, they act from their own screen. When it's an empty seat's turn, you act for it. You can also act for any seat to keep things moving.
  3. Choose a size with the slider or the pot-size buttons, then Bet or Raise. +30s gives a player more time when the shot clock is on.
  4. At showdown the pot is paid out, rake is taken if it's on, and the hand log records everything.

Made a mistake? Undo steps back one action at a time. Cancel this hand (in Table setup) returns every chip to where it was before the deal.

Teaching tools

Reveal all cards
Shows every hand to the students, folded hands too. Press it again to hide them.
Keep my card setup
Deals the same hole cards again next hand, so students can try a different line with the same holding.
Students can open the solver
Never, between hands only, or any time. “Between hands” keeps students from checking the answer in the middle of a decision.
Ask everyone
When a player is to act, press Ask everyone what … should do. Between hands, use Ask a question. The question and choices are filled in for you, and you can edit both. Each student picks an answer privately, with an amount for a bet or raise and an optional reason. You see who has answered but not what they said. Press Reveal answers to show everyone the tally, the amounts and the reasons together.

Odds calculator and poker stove

Both are hidden until you turn them on under Tools at the top. Each person turns them on for themselves, and nobody else sees your tools.

Odds calculator
Fills in the pot and the amount to call from the hand being played, then shows every step as the formula followed by the same formula with the real numbers. It covers pot odds and the equity you need, outs (next card, by the river, and the rules of 2 and 4), whether calling makes money and its expected value, the implied odds you'd need, and what a bluff needs to work. Teachers can pick any player, and outs are counted against everyone's real cards. Untick Use this hand's numbers to try your own.
Poker stove
Works out each player's equity: how often they'd win if all the cards were dealt. Type a hand like AhKh or a range like QQ+,AKs for up to six players, with an optional board and dead cards. Small jobs are checked against every possible board; big ones are estimated from a few million deals. Use the table fills in the hand being played (your own cards if you're a student), and Use the solver's ranges copies the solver's two ranges. Click a player to see what hands they end up with, or send their equity to the odds calculator.

Study a spot in the solver

The solver works out the strongest strategy for both players in a heads-up spot after the flop: how often each hand should check, bet, call, raise or fold.

  1. Play a hand to the flop with two players left. A Solver panel appears under the action bar.
  2. Press Open the flop in the solver (or the turn or river). The window switches to Side by side, with the board, pot, stacks and positions filled in. Use the Table / Side by side / Solver switch at the top any time.
  3. Set the ranges. Both start blank, so nothing gives away what anyone holds. Pick one from Load a starting range, type a range like QQ+,AK,A5s, or paint the 13×13 grid. While Follow the table is on, the board, pot and stacks fill in by themselves as the hand reaches each street, and your ranges stay put.
  4. Pick bet sizes. Quick uses one bet size per street and no raises; Standard adds raises. Both run in the browser.
  5. Press Solve. A flop usually takes a few seconds to a minute.

Reading the answer

The buttons across the top are the actions available, with how often the whole range takes each one. The grid shows every starting hand: the colors split each hand by action, and the height of the fill shows how much of that hand is still in the range. Tap a hand to see each combination with its equity and expected value. Click an action button to see what happens next, and use the trail above to go back.

Following the table

While Follow the table is on, the solver moves along with the real hand: checks and bets at the table move it down the matching line, using the closest bet size. When you pick the turn or river in the solver, the table deals that card next, as long as nobody is holding it.

Studying together

The solver is shared with everyone in the lobby who's allowed to see it. Anyone can edit the ranges and sizes, and everyone moves through the answer together. Press Explore on my own to look around without moving anyone else, and Rejoin the group to come back. Only the computer that pressed Solve does the work, so students on phones can follow along easily.

Keep the page open on the computer that pressed Solve until you're done reviewing. If it leaves, the results go with it, and someone needs to press Solve again.

Notes for each street

Under the solver is a Notes window with a tab for each street: Preflop, Flop, Turn and River. Each tab has room to write and its own blank range grid. Narrow a range as the hand goes on: on the Flop tab, press Start from Preflop to copy the preflop range, then paint out the hands that don't fit. The previous street's range shows faintly underneath, and the line at the top counts the combos left on each street. When the table reaches a new street, its tab opens by itself.

Add a tab with +. Double-click a tab to rename it, drag it to reorder, or close it with ×. The Solver… menu sends a tab's range to the solver, or copies one of the solver's ranges into the tab. Notes are shared with the same people as the solver.

Arrange the panels

In Side by side and Solver views, each panel has a title bar. Drag it by the bar to move it within a column or to the other column. You can also select the ⠿ grip and use the arrow keys. Press ▾ to fold a panel out of the way. Each view remembers its layout on this device, and Reset layout at the top puts everything back.

Big solves

A browser can only use about 4 GB of memory. Turn and river spots and Quick or Standard flops fit easily. Flops with several bet sizes or very wide ranges can need more, and the solver tells you when that happens.

For those, install the free Hot Seat Solver app on your Windows computer. It runs the solve with all of your computer's memory and speed, and everyone in your lobby sees the results as usual. Open it, leave its window open, then press Connect the solver app under the Solve button. If Chrome asks whether the site can access devices on your network, choose Allow.

For students

  1. Open hotseatpoker.com/play (or the link your teacher sent) and type your name.
  2. Press Join as student on your group's lobby, then tap an open seat.
  3. When it's your turn, your action buttons appear under the table. Only you and the teachers can see your cards.
  4. If your teacher allows it, the Solver and Side by side views appear at the top. You can follow the group or explore on your own.
  5. When your teacher asks a question, a poll card pops up in the corner. Pick your answer, add a reason if you like, and send it. Nobody sees it until the teacher reveals the answers.
  6. Open Tools at the top for the odds calculator and poker stove. They use your own cards and only you see them.

Reloading the page or switching apps on your phone is fine: you'll come back to the same seat.

Questions

Does it cost anything?
No. There are no accounts, and solves run on your own computers.
I lost the teacher code.
It can't be recovered. If a browser that used it is still around, it can press Rejoin as teacher. Otherwise start a new lobby.
How do I delete a lobby?
On the table page, press Teach on the lobby, enter the teacher code, and choose Delete this lobby.
Does it work on phones?
Yes, for students and for viewing the solver. Running big solves works best on a laptop or desktop.
Is the solver the same as the paid ones?
It uses postflop-solver, an open-source engine by Wataru Inariba that solves to the same kind of equilibrium. It handles two players after the flop; preflop and multiway spots aren't supported.
